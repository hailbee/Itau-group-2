If both spaces are already spoof-aware and your goal is mainly to make the text embeddings usable in the same “decision geometry” as the image embeddings (so your fake–real similarity ROC-AUC improves or at least doesn’t regress), the best low-tuning choice is a two-term objective: (1) pointwise cosine distillation to align each mapped text vector to its paired image vector (keeps you in the same coordinate system), plus (2) a label-aware similarity term on the mapped text pair cos(pred_fraud, pred_real) trained with BCEWithLogitsLoss (so label=1 pushes that similarity up and label=0 pushes it down), because the first term handles “space alignment” while the second directly optimizes the exact separation your ROC-AUC is computed from; you can keep the distillation weight modest and avoid extra hyperparameters by using a learnable logit scale (instead of tuning temperature) for the BCE term. No — it’s not the only strategy that can do both. “Align spaces” + “preserve/improve spoof separation” can also be done with (a) two-stage training (first pure alignment, then a light label-supervised similarity tuning while freezing most layers), (b) teacher-behavior distillation that matches the teacher’s fake–real similarity score (or its score distribution over candidates) instead of matching vectors, (c) relational distillation (match neighborhood structure / similarity matrix) which often preserves spoof-aware geometry better than pointwise matching, or (d) architecture constraints (orthogonal/low-rank/residual projector) that make alignment happen with minimal distortion. Do the objectives contradict? They can if there’s any mismatch between “what it means to be close in the teacher space” and “what your label wants the fake–real similarity to do” under your text-only mapping (e.g., label=0 pairs that are genuinely similar strings but should be “not a spoof,” or mapping errors that systematically shrink/expand distances); then the label loss will try to warp the mapped space away from strict pointwise alignment. If your image and text spaces are truly consistent and “spoof-aware” in the same sense, they’re mostly compatible—distillation keeps you in the teacher coordinate system, and the label term just nudges the decision boundary—but it’s normal for them to partially conflict, which is why people either (1) gate/weight the label term lightly, (2) do two-stage with freezing, or (3) distill the teacher’s scores rather than forcing exact vector matches.
